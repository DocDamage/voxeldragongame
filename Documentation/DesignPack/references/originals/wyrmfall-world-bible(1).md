# WYRMFALL: The Ten Wyrmlands
### An isekai world bible

---

## The Hook

You die — or just vanish mid-sentence, mid-drive, mid-nothing-special — and wake up inside a ribcage the size of a cathedral, lying in a socket of black stone shaped exactly like a human heart. The bones around you are fossilized. They are also, somehow, still *warm*.

A voice that isn't really a voice tells you what you're lying in: the calcified heart of the **Origin Wyrm**, the first dragon, the dragon every other dragon in this world was born from. It didn't just live a long time — it lived so long it dreamed. Entire ages, entire civilizations, entire *futures*, all passed through its mind like weather. When it finally died, its colossal body didn't rot. It **became the world**. Mountains are its spine. Coastlines are the sweep of its wings. And its ten regions didn't form randomly — each one crystallized around a different age the Wyrm once dreamed. That's why one region is a candlelit medieval kingdom and the next is a zero-g space colony: they aren't different worlds, they're different *dreams* of the same dead god, fossilized side by side.

You've just been bonded to what's left of its heart. That bond is why you — and only you — can reach into the land itself and reshape it: raise soil, calm stone, coax a dead field back to green. But right now that power is barely a flicker. Long ago, ten mortal rulers each found a way to capture and bind a living fragment of the Wyrm — a **Regional Dragon** — chaining a piece of its old authority to themselves and their throne. Until those ten fragments are freed, your bond stays a flicker, and the world keeps dying at the seams (that's why one region is a functioning zombie apocalypse and another is post-nuclear ash — the dream is fraying, not just the land).

To actually become a Warden with real power, you have to go region to region, face down the King (or their champion) currently throttling that land's dragon, **defeat the dragon in honest combat, then earn its loyalty** rather than break it — and each dragon that accepts you hands back a piece of the Wyrm's authority over that *type* of land, permanently.

---

## What Taming Actually Gives You

Every dragon you win over doesn't just become a mount — it unlocks a standing power over that terrain type, which is what ties the "hunt dragons" premise directly to the "farm/fish/craft/chop wood" systems:

| Dragon | Power it unlocks |
|---|---|
| Green Dragon | Accelerated crop growth, calmer seas for fishing/sailing |
| Wooden Dragon | Instant regrowth of chopped timber, living-wood crafting |
| Lava Dragon | Forge-grade smithing heat, stone reshaping |
| White Dragon | Safe travel over ice, preserved/frozen storage |
| Dark Dragon | Safe passage through cursed ground and deep night |
| Zombie Dragon | Cleansing corrupted/irradiated soil |
| Skull Dragon | Command over bone-golem labor, tomb-safe travel |
| Chinese Dragon | Weather-calling for harvests, blessing of cooked food |
| Mecha Dragon | Ley-circuit rerouting — powers crafted tech and flying vehicles |
| Steampunk Dragon | Market/economy leverage — better prices, casino odds, blueprint access |

## The Ten Wyrmlands

### 1. The Gloaming Marches — Dark Dragon
**Geography:** Grew from the Wyrm's ribcage; the region's great cathedral is literally built *inside* a fossilized ribbone.
**Ruler:** Lord Ashgrave, the Umbral Sovereign
**Dragon:** Nyxaroth, chained beneath the cathedral altar
**Setting:** Gothic architecture, a walled cemetery, a working church, the cathedral itself, and — tucked disturbingly beside the graveyard — a rusted, decades-abandoned playground.
**Inhabitants:** Night-clergy, grave-wardens, wandering wizards who study the Wyrm's death
**Notable threat:** **Count Malvaine**, a centuries-old noble who drinks the last of the Wyrm's "dream-blood" to stay young — classic vampire-lord energy. Secondary: **the Hollow Twins**, two small ghosts who only ever want one more turn on the swings.

### 2. The Zenith Spire — Mecha Dragon
**Geography:** A fragment of a future the Wyrm foresaw, crystallized whole — towers, transit rails, and a full orbital colony.
**Ruler:** Overking Vantrix-9, the Iron Monarch (part machine, part original Wyrm-dreamer)
**Dragon:** an armored construct-dragon of the same make as the city
**Setting:** Flying cars, robot citizens/laborers, a low-orbit space colony reachable by dragon-flight.
**Inhabitants:** Engineer-knights, drone-rangers, palace technicians
**Notable threat:** **The Broodmother Cipher**, a parasitic hive-organism that got into the colony's vents and is quietly replacing crew one pod at a time.

### 3. Cogspire Harbor — Steampunk Dragon
**Geography:** A dreamed industrial age — brass, steam, and smog stacked over a working harbor.
**Ruler:** Baron Feist Cogwell, the Brass Baron
**Dragon:** Cogfang, harnessed to the city's central engine
**Setting:** A neon-lit casino district, a foggy noir waterfront (great for a detective side-plot), a boxing gym in the dockyards, taverns everywhere.
**Inhabitants:** Private eyes, dockworkers, merchant-rangers, casino staff
**Notable threat:** **The House Mark**, an unidentified figure in a featureless mask who kills high rollers at the casino one by one. Secondary: **Chef Aurelio Vane**, a beloved restaurateur whose signature dish nobody should ask too many questions about.

### 4. The Cinderreach — Lava Dragon
**Geography:** Formed from the Wyrm's forge-glands; the ground still runs hot.
**Ruler:** Magnarok, the Ember King
**Dragon:** Pyraxis
**Setting:** Volcanic forges, a gladiator-style fighting pit built into the rock (your boxing gym, fire-themed), a dungeon carved beneath the throne.
**Inhabitants:** Smith-knights, fire priests, arena handlers
**Notable threat:** **The Flayed Choir**, a cult that runs the under-dungeon as a "temple of pain," led by an overseer who believes suffering is devotion.

### 5. The Verdant Reach — Green Dragon
**Geography:** The Wyrm's belly, turned fertile jungle-sea.
**Ruler:** Queen Meridess Tidecaller, the Coral Queen — a former pirate captain who took the throne by conquest
**Dragon:** Verdance
**Setting:** Tropical islands, working pirate ships, fishing, swimming, jungle farmland.
**Inhabitants:** Pirate crews, sea-rangers, farmers on the terraced coast
**Notable threats:** **The Counselor**, a masked figure who surfaces from the lake shallows and never speaks — bad news for anyone swimming after dark. Deeper in the jungle interior, something else hunts that's never fully seen, only heard clicking in the canopy.

### 6. Frosthold — White Dragon
**Geography:** The Wyrm's frozen wing, now a classic medieval kingdom.
**Ruler:** King Alaric Frostborne
**Dragon:** Frostmane
**Setting:** Castle, tavern, a village square used as a playground/festival ground, a dungeon beneath the keep — the most "storybook fantasy" region of the ten.
**Inhabitants:** Knights, court wizards, priests, palace staff — the classic RPG cast lives here
**Notable threat:** **Ser Corvyn the Cursed**, a knight who turns into something else on the full moon and has been quietly disappearing patrols for a year. In the dungeon below, condemned prisoners report the same recurring nightmare right before they're found dead in their sleep.

### 7. The Ashen Wastes — Zombie Dragon
**Geography:** The wound where the Wyrm's death actually began — the one region visibly still rotting.
**Ruler:** The Rotking, once a living monarch named Devrin, now something that refuses to finish dying
**Dragon:** Rotwing
**Setting:** Full zombie-apocalypse ruins, a sealed nuclear bunker beneath the old palace, scavenger economy.
**Inhabitants:** Survivor camps, scrap-crafters, bunker holdouts
**Notable threat:** **Doctor Hollowmend**, the scientist-king's old physician, still stitching salvaged bodies into "improvements" in a ruined lab. Inside the bunker: a survivor who's been "rescuing" travelers for years and has never once let one leave.

### 8. The Bonelands — Skull Dragon
**Geography:** Literally built in and around the Wyrm's fossilized skull.
**Ruler:** Kael Marrow, the Ossuary Lord
**Dragon:** Ossuroth
**Setting:** Bone deserts, mausoleum-cities, a dungeon of tombs.
**Inhabitants:** Death-cult priests, bone-carvers, hunters
**Notable threat:** **The Skinning Man**, who runs an isolated homestead at the desert's edge that travelers are warned never to stop at. Guarding the oldest tomb is a wrapped, silent guardian who's been "protecting" the same treasure for three thousand years and does not care that its king is dead.

### 9. The Jade Peaks — Chinese Dragon
**Geography:** Where the Wyrm's mane became mist-wrapped mountains.
**Ruler:** Emperor Wei Longzhu, styled the Jade Emperor
**Dragon:** Jadefang
**Setting:** Temple terraces, farming valleys, martial dojos, festival cooking.
**Inhabitants:** Monks, farmers, martial rangers, palace scholars
**Notable threat:** **The Well-Bound**, a spirit sealed into an old bronze mirror generations ago who "shows" herself to anyone who looks too long. A young temple disciple, bullied for years by the court, is starting to move things without touching them — she could become an ally or the region's next disaster, depending on how she's treated.

### 10. The Hallowwood — Wooden Dragon
**Geography:** The Wyrm's petrified tail, grown over into deep forest.
**Ruler:** Osk Grownroot, the Timber Warden
**Dragon:** Grovemaw
**Setting:** Farmland, logging camps, a tavern, and the rotting remains of a traveling carnival that never left.
**Inhabitants:** Foresters, farmers, carnival stragglers
**Notable threat:** **The Hollow Harvestman**, a masked figure who's stalked the logging trails for decades and is never seen moving, only suddenly *there*. In the carnival ruins, something that isn't really one shape feeds on whatever you're most afraid of. A toymaker's cottage nearby holds a puppet that finishes projects its maker never got to.

## The Recurring Nemesis

One figure isn't tied to any single region: **the Rustbound Judge**, a silent, faceless executioner who appears — always in a dungeon, always without warning — as a kind of living verdict on whether you deserve the Wyrm's power. It never speaks, never hurries, and is never fully beaten, just outrun. Good candidate for a boss that reappears, stronger, every couple of regions.

## A Bigger Twist (optional)

If you want a final-act reveal: the ten Kings didn't independently discover how to bind a dragon fragment. Someone taught all of them, generations apart, using the same ritual — because that person is the one who actually killed the Origin Wyrm in the first place, and has been quietly keeping its remains fractured ever since so it can never wake up and hold anyone accountable. That gives you a "final region" beyond the ten: the actual corpse-site of the Wyrm, and a villain who's been offscreen the whole game.

## Rough Campaign Shape

- **Act 1** — Wake in the heart-chamber, tame your first 1–2 dragons (Wooden and Green work well as "gentle" starting regions), learn the Wyrm-dream truth.
- **Act 2** — The other seven regions, escalating strangeness, the Rustbound Judge's first two appearances, hints that something orchestrated all ten Kings.
- **Act 3** — Confront the true architect of the Wyrm's death at the corpse-site itself; final choice of whether to fully restore the Wyrm (unify the ten dreams back into one world) or leave the regions independent (preserve them as they are).
